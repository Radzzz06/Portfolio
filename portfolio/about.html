> WHO IS RADZZZZ?  

TESTIMONY.TXT

I'm Radhika, and if you asked my family, they'd tell you I've been practicing for a Broadway lead since the day I was born—overdramatic, a total chatterbox, and always somewhere in the middle of a dance routine, a song, or a painting. Coming from a small town, I hit the hostel circuit in 8th grade, trading home comforts for a lifetime of wild memories.

Growing up in a house of doctors, everyone expected me to follow the family 'script,' but I took a major plot twist into Computer Science Engineering. Why? Because I asked 'How does a computer work?' once, found out about processors, and my brain basically went: 'WHATTTT??' I had to know more. JEE and the academic grind might have turned down my volume and made me a bit shy for a moment, but deep down, I'm still that same artsy, tech-obsessed girl waiting for the next beat to drop so I can start dancing again.

If you had told my younger, artsy self that I'd end up as a 'tech girly,' I probably would've laughed. But something about the logic pulled me in. Then came IIIT Hyderabad and lets be real, I wasn't even remotely ready for the sheer weight of it. There were days where the struggle felt like a heavy fog, especially after a few rough years of life just being... well, life.

But then, in those rare quiet moments when I'd actually stop to breathe and look at a CPU, it would hit me: Billions of transistors? All working together just so I can click a button? How does a website even exist out of thin air? Suddenly, the struggle shifted. It wasn't just about surviving the workload anymore; it was about the sheer 'WHATTT??' factor of how cool this stuff actually is. Now, I'm not just learning about it I'm building my own. Between the new faces, the new city, and the rollercoaster of emotions, I've realized that while the struggle is real, the view from the other side of a solved problem is totally worth it.

  • YEAR 2024

    Not a milestone but yeah Prefrontal Cortex development

    For years, I was the girl who did everything—the sports, the grades, the adventures. But by the time I hit 11th grade, the engine just... stalled. I was burnt out. The JEE pressure wasn't a mountain I could rappel down; it felt like a weight I had to carry, and for the first time in my life, my natural 'overdramatic' spark went dim. Scoring a 95 percentile and missing the Advanced cutoff felt like a total crash. While my friends were celebrating and posting their wins, I was stuck in a quiet room. I felt like a version of myself I didn't recognize. But that 'useless' feeling? It was actually the system rebooting. I had to sit in that silence to realize that my worth wasn't a percentile. It was the moment I decided to stop wondering 'what I had become' and started figuring out 'who I wanted to be next.' It was time for a hard restart.

  • YEAR 2025

    AIR 2711

    The drop year was a literal forge. I went from feeling useless to standing in the middle of a 'soul-burning' coaching center, pushing myself until I hit a 99.83 percentile and AIR 2711. While I should have been focusing on JEE Advanced, I was fighting just to breathe. I had to undergo surgeries that took my focus away from the exam and put it entirely on survival.Fumbling Advanced felt like a second heartbreak, a deep sense of lostlessness. But then, my dad suggested one last 'What if?'—the IIIT Hyderabad form. BOOM!! The selection happened. Landing at IIIT-H was like being dropped into the deep end of the ocean. Semester 1 was a rough blur of catching up and finding my footing but slowly, the 'How does it work?' girl is coming back. I'm learning the rhythm of this place, one transistor and one line of code at a time.
